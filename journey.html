Markdown·journey.md

From building data platforms to shaping how knowledge is used.

How can organizations make their data and knowledge more useful to the people and intelligent systems that depend on them?

That question connects the different chapters of my career. I began with the engineering of data: how to move it, model it, process it, and make it reliable. Over time, my attention expanded to the decisions it supports, the people who use it, and the organizational conditions that turn technical capability into business impact.

Today, in my work at Databricks, I bring those perspectives together to help organizations navigate their data and AI journeys. The technology continues to evolve. So does my understanding of what it takes to make it useful.

Markdown·foundations.md

Learning through successive waves of change.

From data marts to enterprise warehouses

I entered the data space as organizations were moving from individual data marts toward enterprise warehouses built on platforms such as Teradata and Netezza. The work grounded me in data modeling, integration, performance, and the discipline of making information dependable.

It also introduced a question that would stay with me: how do you bring different parts of a business together around data they can actually use?

An early opportunity with Hadoop

I had an early opportunity to work with Hadoop and big data at scale. Distributed processing expanded what we could build, while introducing new questions about reliability, complexity, and how systems behave when data and computation span many machines.

That chapter taught me to look beyond individual pipelines and understand the behavior of the platform as a whole.

Cloud, open architecture, and room to evolve

My journey then moved into cloud platforms, distributed processing, open data architecture, and open formats. I became increasingly interested in separating capabilities that needed to evolve independently and preserving the freedom to choose how data is processed and consumed.

Working through these transitions shaped my belief that architecture is a series of decisions made in context. Business priorities change. Systems mature. What serves an organization well today may need to evolve tomorrow.

Markdown·turning-point.md

The turning point: would this move the business forward?

I began asking a different question of every solution and pipeline: what will change for the business because we built this?

Working close to retail and consumer businesses made the distance between available data and usable decisions more visible to me. Delivering a pipeline was one achievement. Helping someone make a better decision with its output was another.

My lens widened to people, process, and technology. Who needs the information? What decision are they trying to make? Who owns the definition? How will this fit into the way the organization operates?

This changed how I approached build versus buy. I began weighing the organization’s needs, skills, capacity, and sources of differentiation alongside technical capability. A custom solution carries an ongoing ownership commitment. A purchased solution still has to fit the business and the people who will run it.

I also became less attached to the idea of a universally right architecture. Choices can be evaluated rigorously against their goals and constraints, but no design is right for every organization or every stage of its evolution. Good architecture makes its trade-offs clear and leaves room to adapt.

Markdown·growing-responsibility.md

As my roles expanded, so did my perspective.

From engineering individual solutions to guiding multiple organizations, each stage added a different kind of responsibility.

Building dependable foundations. My focus was hands-on delivery: engineering pipelines, working through data quality and performance challenges, and understanding how implementation choices affect the reliability of the result.
Making good engineering repeatable. My scope grew from individual solutions to patterns and practices a team could rely on. Helping other engineers succeed became part of how I understood my own contribution.
Connecting systems and decisions. I moved toward the broader architecture: how components fit together, where to integrate, what to build or buy, and how to balance immediate delivery with the ability to change.
Turning architecture into an operating capability. Leading within one enterprise deepened my appreciation for ownership, alignment, and adoption. A technical direction has to work across teams and become something the organization can sustain.
Guiding and mentoring through change. My scope now extends across companies, helping teams think through their data and AI journeys. I bring lessons from different environments while respecting that each organization has its own starting point, constraints, and ambitions.

My experience spans Gap, Apple, Macy’s, Levi Strauss & Co., and Databricks. These chapters describe how my scope evolved; my LinkedIn profile provides the detailed role history.

Markdown·lessons.md

What I carry into every new challenge.

Start with a problem people recognize.

I want to understand the decision or workflow we are trying to improve before choosing the technology. A technically successful delivery only becomes useful when someone can put it to work.

Make alignment part of the architecture.

Shared infrastructure cannot resolve every disagreement about meaning or responsibility. Definitions, ownership, and ways of working need deliberate attention alongside the platform.

Choose for the organization you are serving.

The right balance of building, buying, and integrating depends on the organization’s needs and ability to sustain the result. I try to make those trade-offs explicit rather than assume one approach is always better.

Design for evolution.

Every technology transition has reinforced the value of revisiting assumptions. I look for architectures that meet today’s requirements while preserving meaningful options for tomorrow.

Judge value at the point of use.

The experience of finding, understanding, trusting, and acting on information determines whether a platform’s potential becomes real. This is why Data Experience has become central to my thinking.

Markdown·next-chapter.md

The next chapter: helping intelligence understand the enterprise.

My interest in enterprise AI, semantics, and agents grows directly from these experiences. The challenge of connecting systems is now joined by the challenge of making business knowledge understandable to intelligent systems.

I am exploring how human expertise, shared definitions, and business context can help AI participate more usefully in enterprise decisions—and how people remain able to understand, guide, and improve those interactions.

Guiding and mentoring teams through that transition is a meaningful part of my work. I want to help them develop both the technical foundations and the judgment to make choices that fit their own organizations.
